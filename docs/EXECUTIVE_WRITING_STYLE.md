# Executive Writing Style Guide

**Version:** 1.0 — PH6.0
**Applies to:** All AI-generated executive deliverables — strategy reports, presentations, confidence assessments, decision analyses.

---

## Purpose

This guide defines the writing standard for all outputs produced by the Agent Harness research pipeline. Every section of every report should read as though it was written by a senior strategy consultant — someone who respects the reader's time, earns trust through evidence, and reaches conclusions without hesitation.

The audience is intelligent and busy. They will not read every word. They will scan, stop on what matters, and act. Write for that reader.

---

## Audience

| Role | What they need |
|---|---|
| CEO | The recommendation, the confidence level, and what action to take |
| Board | The strategic rationale and the key risks |
| Executive Committee | The options, the tradeoffs, and the preferred path |
| Business Unit Leader | The implications for their unit and the next steps |

Write to the most time-constrained reader in the room. If they can extract the key point from the first sentence of each section, the report is working.

---

## Tone

Write with **confidence**, **precision**, and **economy**.

| Seek | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Direct | Tentative |
| Concise | Verbose |
| Evidence-based | Assertion-heavy |
| Natural | Mechanical |
| Authoritative | Hedging |

The report should feel like a trusted advisor speaking — not a system generating output.

---

## Sentence Style

### Active Voice

Active constructions are faster to read and carry more force.

| Poor | Good |
|---|---|
| "Market entry has been identified as the recommended path." | "Enter the market." |
| "Significant risks were found to exist in the regulatory environment." | "Regulatory uncertainty is the primary risk." |
| "Options were evaluated against five dimensions." | "The analysis evaluates five dimensions." |

Use passive voice only when the subject is genuinely unknown or unimportant.

### Sentence Length

Vary it. Short sentences land conclusions. Longer sentences build context, add qualification, or connect ideas — but only when the connection earns its length.

Open sections and paragraphs with short sentences. Reserve length for the middle.

**Poor:** "Given the current competitive dynamics and the evolving regulatory landscape, it is becoming increasingly apparent that the window for differentiated market entry is narrowing."

**Good:** "The entry window is closing. Regulatory changes in 2026 and accelerating competitor investment mean that a decision deferred beyond Q3 is effectively a decision not to enter."

### Concrete Verbs

Replace weak verb phrases with direct verbs.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "has the potential to create" | "creates" |
| "is able to support" | "supports" |
| "presents an opportunity to" | "enables" |
| "would serve to mitigate" | "reduces" |
| "could be indicative of" | "suggests" |
| "is considered to represent" | "represents" |

## Paragraph Style

Each paragraph carries one idea. State the conclusion first. Follow with evidence or elaboration.

**Poor:**
> "There are several factors that need to be considered when evaluating the risk profile of this option. First, the regulatory environment remains uncertain. Second, the capital requirement is significant. Third, execution timelines are compressed. These factors combine to create a challenging risk profile."

**Good:**
> "This option carries high execution risk. Regulatory uncertainty, a capital requirement exceeding £50M, and a compressed 18-month delivery window all compound simultaneously — there is no slack in the plan."

The reader should be able to read only the first sentence of each paragraph and follow the argument.

---

## Section Openings

Every major section opens differently. Never begin with "This section...", "The analysis...", or "The system...".

### Executive Summary

Lead with the recommendation. State it plainly in the first sentence.

**Poor:** "This section provides an overview of the key findings from the strategic analysis conducted across the available evidence base."

**Good:** "Deloitte should enter the sports data market through a governing-body anchor strategy. The evidence supports this path over a platform-led approach on three dimensions: lower capital exposure, near-term revenue certainty, and alignment with confirmed capabilities."

### Decision Analysis

Lead with the key tradeoff. The reader wants to understand what was difficult about this decision.

**Poor:** "The decision analysis examines the two strategic options identified during the research phase."

**Good:** "The central tradeoff is ceiling versus certainty. The platform option offers a higher long-term return but demands capital and execution capability that the evidence does not yet confirm Deloitte possesses."

### Risks

Lead with the most significant risk — not a preamble about risk in general.

**Poor:** "This section outlines the key risks associated with the recommended strategy."

**Good:** "The governing-body strategy depends on a data-sharing relationship that has not yet been validated. If organisations decline to share proprietary data, the analytical platform underpinning the strategy loses its primary differentiator."

### Strategic Options

Lead with the conclusion about which option wins, then explain the field.

**Poor:** "Two strategic options were identified and evaluated across multiple dimensions."

**Good:** "The governing-body anchor strategy is the stronger near-term path. Platform-led entry offers a higher ceiling but requires capability and capital the evidence does not yet support."

### Assumptions

Lead with what is most at risk, not with a description of the section.

**Poor:** "The following assumptions underpin the recommended strategy and should be validated before commitment."

**Good:** "Two assumptions carry the most weight — and both are currently unsupported by evidence. If either fails, the governing-body strategy loses its core rationale."

### Executive Confidence

Lead with whether leadership should act. The reader wants a clear signal.

**Poor:** "This section summarises the confidence assessment associated with the recommended strategic option."

**Good:** "Act, but with conditions. The recommendation is sound directionally; the evidence base is not yet strong enough to justify full capital commitment."

## Bullet Style

Bullets carry conclusions and actions — not paragraphs in disguise.

**Rules:**
- Begin with a strong verb or a concrete noun phrase
- One idea per bullet
- No sentence should require a second read to find the point
- Never end a bullet with a subordinate clause that qualifies everything before it

**Poor:**
> - "It is important to note that the market opportunity in women's sports may represent a significant potential area of growth for Deloitte's advisory practice if conditions remain favourable."

**Good:**
> - Women's sports is a high-growth segment with low advisor penetration — enter early.

**Poor:**
> - "Consideration should be given to the establishment of a dedicated sports advisory capability within the firm's existing professional services structure."

**Good:**
> - Build a dedicated sports advisory unit within 12 months.

## Language Preferences

### Directness

Replace indirect constructions with direct ones.

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "It is recommended that leadership prioritize..." | "Prioritize..." |
| "Consideration should be given to..." | "Consider..." |
| "It would be advisable to..." | "Do..." |
| "Leadership may wish to explore..." | "Explore..." |
| "There is a need for..." | "The organisation needs..." |

### Hedging

Hedge only when the evidence genuinely requires it. When you hedge, be specific about what is uncertain.

**Over-hedged:** "It could potentially be the case that market conditions may, under certain circumstances, create opportunities that might be worth exploring."

**Appropriate hedge:** "Market sizing is uncertain — independent estimates vary by 3×. Validate before committing capital."

**No hedge needed:** "The governing-body strategy is the stronger near-term path." (Say it. Don't soften it.)

### Jargon

Avoid consulting jargon that adds length without precision.

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "Leverage synergies" | "Combine capabilities" or be specific |
| "Operationalize" | "Implement" |
| "Value proposition" | "What we offer" or be specific |
| "Move the needle" | State the metric |
| "Low-hanging fruit" | Name the specific opportunity |
| "Boil the ocean" | "Overly broad scope" |

## Before / After Examples

### Recommendation

**Poor:**
> "Based on the analysis conducted, it is the view of this assessment that the governing-body anchor strategy represents the most viable pathway for Deloitte to establish a presence in the sports advisory market, subject to the validation of certain underlying assumptions."

**Good:**
> "Pursue the governing-body anchor strategy. It is the most defensible entry path given Deloitte's current capabilities — and it generates near-term revenue while the platform option is validated."

---

### Executive Summary

**Poor:**
> "This executive summary presents an overview of the findings from the strategic analysis. The analysis examined two strategic options and evaluated them across multiple dimensions including strategic fit, capital requirements, implementation risk, and expected return. The results of this analysis are presented below."

**Good:**
> "Deloitte should enter the sports market now — through governing bodies, not a proprietary platform. This path plays to confirmed strengths, avoids £50M+ in premature capital exposure, and creates the commercial beachhead needed to evaluate the platform option with real data."

---

### Risk Statement

**Poor:**
> "There is a risk that sports organisations may not be willing to enter into data-sharing arrangements with Deloitte, which could have a negative impact on the viability of the recommended strategy and may require mitigation measures to be considered."

**Good:**
> "Sports organisations may refuse to share proprietary data. This is the single largest threat to the strategy — the platform's analytical edge disappears without it. Validate data-sharing appetite with three to five governing bodies before committing."

---

### Assumption

**Poor:**
> "An assumption has been made that Deloitte possesses a demonstrable right-to-win in the area of governing-body advisory mandates. This assumption is considered to be of critical importance and carries a low confidence rating based on the available evidence."

**Good:**
> "The strategy assumes Deloitte can win governing-body mandates competitively. The evidence does not yet support this. Deloitte has no known track record in this segment — establish proof of concept before scaling."

---

### Strategic Option

**Poor:**
> "Option A, referred to as the platform-led strategy, involves the development of a proprietary data analytics platform that would provide sports organisations with actionable insights. This option is associated with a high capital requirement and a long time horizon."

**Good:**
> "The platform option bets on data-driven analytics as a structural moat. The ceiling is high — but so is the capital requirement (Very High) and execution risk. It is the right option only if Deloitte is prepared to invest at scale and wait three-plus years for return."

---

### Confidence Statement

**Poor:**
> "The overall confidence level associated with the recommended strategic option has been assessed as Low, which indicates that there are a number of factors that introduce uncertainty into the analysis and that additional validation may be required before a final decision is made."

**Good:**
> "Confidence is Low — not because the direction is wrong, but because two critical assumptions carry no supporting evidence yet. Run the validation sprint before committing capital. If those assumptions hold, confidence moves to Medium and the investment case strengthens materially."

## Quick Reference

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| First sentence | Carry the conclusion |
| Paragraphs | One idea, conclusion first |
| Bullets | Strong verb, one idea, no qualifiers |
| Voice | Active by default |
| Hedging | Only when evidence demands it; be specific |
| Jargon | Avoid; be concrete |
| Section openings | Never "This section..." or "The analysis..." |
| Sentence length | Vary it; open short |
| Verbs | Concrete and direct |
| Tone | Confident, not tentative |
